Law No. 4875 on Foreign Direct Investment gives foreign and Turkish entrepreneurs equal rights: a foreigner can open a company in Turkey in any form provided for by the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 — including without a Turkish partner.

Restrictions and requirements for foreigners

Some fields are closed or restricted for foreigners:

  • dentistry and veterinary medicine;
  • legal practice and notarial services;
  • private security services;
  • cabotage shipping and certain types of maritime and tourism activities.

Many activities require a licence — for example, real estate agencies and beauty salons. If you plan not only to own the company but also to work in it, you will need a work permit. The classic condition for it is that the company employs five Turkish citizens with an official salary and insurance (since October 2024 there are exceptions — see below).

Free economic zones

Free economic zones (FEZ) operate in Adana, Antalya, Izmir, Mardin, Mersin, Istanbul, Trabzon, Erzurum and other regions. Advantages of operating in a FEZ:

  • exemption from customs duties, VAT, special consumption tax and payroll taxes;
  • supplies to both the domestic and foreign markets;
  • unlimited storage of goods in warehouses;
  • reduced land rent and electricity tariffs;
  • no currency restrictions and no limits on the volume of investment;
  • convenient transport logistics.

Operating in a FEZ requires a licence from the Ministry of Trade. At the same time, you can open a company in any city — not necessarily in a free zone.

Step-by-step guide: how to open a company in Turkey

Step 1. Choose the company type

  • Sole proprietorship (şahıs şirketi) — available to foreigners with a residence permit; income tax on a progressive scale from 15 to 40%.
  • Limited company (limited şirketi) — the most popular form among foreigners. From 1 to 50 shareholders (individuals or legal entities), the director can be a foreigner. Minimum share capital — 50,000 TL, corporate tax — 25%.
  • Joint-stock company (anonim şirketi) — shareholders are liable only to the extent of their shares, board members can be foreigners. Minimum capital — 250,000 TL, corporate tax — 25%.

Foreign companies can also open a subsidiary limited or joint-stock company in Turkey, a branch (with the right to carry out commercial activity) or a liaison office (without that right).

Step 2. Find an accountant

Every company must keep accounts and file reports through a licensed accountant (mali müşavir), who also helps with registration. You can hire an in-house accountant or outsource.

Step 3. Obtain a tax number

The founders obtain a Turkish tax number (vergi numarası) at the tax office or online — on the İnteraktif Vergi Dairesi portal (dijital.gib.gov.tr).

Step 4. Prepare the documents

The basic registration package:

  • passport with an apostille and a notarised translation into Turkish (original and 2 copies);
  • photographs of the founders and directors;
  • specimen signatures of the founders (2 copies);
  • the company's articles of association, certified by a notary;
  • a Turkish tax number;
  • a registered address — an office or a virtual office.

If the founder is a legal entity, you will also need an extract from the register of the country of registration, a resolution to open a subsidiary in Turkey, a resolution appointing a representative and a power of attorney. All foreign documents must be apostilled and translated with notarisation.

Step 5. Register in MERSİS and with the Chamber of Commerce

The articles of association and registration forms are completed in Turkish in the central MERSİS system — the company is assigned a number required for paying in the capital. The signatures of the persons representing the company are certified at the trade registry, after which the registration application is submitted. The trade registry registers the company and certifies its books, then the company is registered with the Chamber of Commerce. The registration notice is published in the Trade Registry Gazette (Ticaret Sicili Gazetesi).

Step 6. Open a bank account

The bank will need the incorporation documents, the Chamber of Commerce registration certificate, the tax number and proof of the registered address. The exact list depends on the bank.

Step 7. Pay in the share capital

The capital can be paid in instalments, but at least 25% must be paid before registration is completed. A fee to the Competition Authority (Rekabet Kurumu) of 0.04% of the capital is also paid at registration.

Step 8. Register for tax

After registering with the Chamber of Commerce, the company registers with the tax office and receives its corporate tax number. This is done by the accountant.

Step 9. Register employees

All employees must be registered with the Turkish social security system (SGK).

Step 10. Legalise your stay

A foreign founder and director need a residence permit and health insurance, and a work permit to work in the company.

New employment rules from 1 October 2024

Turkey has simplified work permits for foreigners who start a business:

  • with an investment of $100,000 or more in a Turkish business, a work permit can be obtained without hiring five Turkish citizens, and the spouse and children under 18 can obtain residence permits;
  • if you have lived in Turkey legally for 3 of the last 5 years, you can hire your first three foreign employees without the five-citizen requirement; after that, the number of foreigners must not exceed the number of Turkish employees;
  • in a number of industries, the minimum wage for foreign workers is equal to the minimum wage for citizens;
  • foreigners whose parents or children are Turkish citizens receive a permit without additional conditions;
  • IT companies can hire their first two foreign employees on simplified terms.
