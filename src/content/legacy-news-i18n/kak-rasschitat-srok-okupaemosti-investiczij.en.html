When investing in any financial project, every investor first tries to estimate how quickly the investment will start generating profit and bring the expected return. The key parameter here is the payback period.

What the payback period is

It is a financial indicator that defines the time needed to recover the invested funds through the cash flows the investment generates. It allows investors to assess the efficiency of their investments and compare different ways of placing capital.

Who needs to calculate the payback period

  • Investors use it to determine how quickly they can recover their investment and start making a profit. This helps them make informed decisions about allocating capital and choosing projects.
  • Entrepreneurs use it to analyse projects and business ideas before deciding to implement them, to understand how efficiently funds will be used.
  • Financial analysts calculate payback to assess the financial efficiency of companies, investment projects and strategies, and to give recommendations on financial planning.
  • Banks, investment funds and other financial institutions use payback calculations when deciding on loans, investments and portfolio management.

What data you need

Let's look at a simple example. Suppose you invested $100,000 in a real estate construction project, and the annual income from it is $20,000. The payback period is the ratio of the amount invested to the annual income: $100,000 / $20,000 = 5 years. So you will recover the invested funds 5 years after the start of the investment.

This example uses a simple formula that takes into account only the initial investment and the annual income:

Simple payback period = Investment / Annual income

In practice, a discounted calculation is used more often. It takes into account:

  • Investment — the amount invested, including the cost of the assets and related expenses.
  • Return — the expected income over the period: rent, dividends, capital gains and so on.
  • Investment term — the period over which the calculation is made.
  • Discount rate — the interest rate used to bring future cash flows to their present value.

The discounted calculation gives a more accurate assessment of financial efficiency and helps investors make better-informed decisions about allocating capital.
