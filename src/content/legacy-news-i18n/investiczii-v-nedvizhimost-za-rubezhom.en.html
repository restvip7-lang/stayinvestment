Investing in foreign real estate is becoming increasingly attractive and accessible to investors of all levels. It solves several tasks at once: diversifying an investment portfolio, creating a stable long-term source of income or laying the groundwork for future relocation.

But such investments can be risky, especially if you do not know the housing market of a particular country and its nuances. It is important to understand in advance how to invest in property abroad correctly in order to earn a stable profit from it.

What sets investing in foreign real estate apart

  • Global economic instability and volatile financial markets push investors to look for more reliable assets. Property abroad is a long-term investment that can provide a steady income stream and protect capital from inflation.
  • Easy travel makes it faster and simpler for investors to view properties, negotiate and close deals abroad.
  • Investors can choose from a wide range of properties, from apartments in resort areas to commercial property in large cities.

In many countries that allow foreign investors to earn from real estate, housing costs less today than in the large cities of many investors' home countries. Another factor is the opportunity to obtain a residence permit, citizenship or simply a second home in a region with a more pleasant climate.

Why investing in real estate abroad pays off

  • Spreading investments across different assets and regions reduces risk and makes a portfolio more stable. Investments abroad reduce dependence on a single market.
  • Many regions and even entire countries offer high rental yields and steady demand for rental housing, which gives investors predictable income from rent.
  • Some markets show fast growth in housing prices, which increases the value of the investment over time and brings profit on resale.
  • Many countries, Turkey in particular, offer foreign investors additional benefits, such as the possibility of obtaining a residence permit or citizenship and tax incentives.

What risks to consider

As with any investment, when buying property abroad it is important to assess a number of risks:

  1. Market risk. Changes in the economy, housing demand, inflation and other factors can affect the value and yield of the property.
  2. Political risk. Political instability or changes in legislation can harm the investment climate. New taxes, restrictions or nationalisation of property are among the reasons to choose the country carefully.
  3. Hidden tax obligations. Countries have different tax rates and rules for owning and letting property. In some countries, the tax on purchase alone can reach 20% of the price, and it is paid by the buyer.
  4. Difficulties in managing property abroad. Failure to find tenants, late rent payments, ineffective management or unexpected maintenance costs reduce the return.

The yield of investments in foreign real estate

Like any other asset, investments in foreign real estate carry risk, and specific amounts cannot be predicted precisely. Market conditions, macroeconomic trends, political stability and other factors affect the yield, and much can change over time.

That is why we recommend consulting professionals, analysing the risks thoroughly and making decisions based on fundamental factors and the goals of the investment.

Where it pays to buy property

Several countries stand out where buying real estate generates income:

  • Spain. Popular with foreign investors thanks to its pleasant climate, rich culture and many opportunities for holidays and rentals.
  • Greece. Beautiful beaches, islands and historic sights make it an attractive destination for investors interested in holiday rentals and buying homes.
  • UAE. A promising destination with developing infrastructure and attractive investment opportunities.
  • Turkey. Perhaps the most popular destination for foreign investors. High rental income potential, stable profit and prospects for price growth are the main, but not the only, factors.
