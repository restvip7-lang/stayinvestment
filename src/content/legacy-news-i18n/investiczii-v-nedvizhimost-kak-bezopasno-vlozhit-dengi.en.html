Real estate has long proven to be one of the most reliable and profitable ways to preserve and grow capital. Yet despite its obvious advantages, the safety of the investment remains a key question for many investors. How do you make sure the money you invest brings the expected return and is not at risk? What precautions help minimise risks and ensure long-term success?

In this article, we look at the key aspects of investing in real estate safely.

Top 5 options for investing money

When it comes to investing money safely, real estate is among the leaders. It is one of the most reliable ways to preserve and grow capital. But which options exist on the market, and which are the most promising? Here are the top 5 opportunities for those who have decided to invest in real estate.

1. Residential complexes under construction. Investing at the construction stage is a popular choice. By buying early, you can get the best price per square metre and then sell at market price or rent the property out. This suits those who are ready to wait until the project is completed and want to profit from the difference in value. Investors can usually expect value growth of 20% to 40% by completion.

2. Commercial property. Investing in offices, shopping centres or hotels is another way to earn stable income. Rent for commercial property is usually higher than for residential, which makes this type of investment especially attractive. Rental yield can be 6–12% per year, depending on the location and quality of the property.

3. Coastal property. Buying resort property, for example by the sea, can be an excellent asset. Such properties are in high demand both for rental and for sale, and have long-term potential for value growth. In popular tourist areas, the yield from short-term rental can reach 10–15% per year.

4. Renovation and resale. Investors can also buy old buildings, renovate them and sell them. This requires certain knowledge and experience but can bring significant profit.

5. Houses and land. Demand for country houses and plots has grown noticeably in recent years: people increasingly value living close to nature, away from the city noise. Investing in property outside the city can be a good investment, especially in developing areas. Growth in value depends on infrastructure and demand, but usually ranges from 10% to 20% per year.

Real estate investments can be profitable if you choose the property wisely. Before deciding, study the market carefully and assess your resources and goals. And remember to choose a reliable partner for your transactions.

Types of investment risk

Every investor should be prepared for the unexpected in order to minimise potential losses. Here are the main types of risk you may face when investing in real estate.

  1. Market risks. These relate to changes in the market value of property. External economic factors such as recession, inflation or changes in interest rates can affect property values. The market can be cyclical, and periods of growth can be followed by periods of decline.
  2. Construction risks. Investing in properties under construction carries risks such as construction delays, rising material costs, problems with contractors or missed completion dates. These factors can affect the timing of the return and the total amount invested.
  3. Rental risks. If you plan to rent the property out, consider the difficulty of finding tenants, the risk of non-payment and the need for repairs and maintenance. It is important to vet tenants carefully and sign contracts that protect your interests — or work with companies that take over all property management obligations. Stay Investment, for example, provides such a service.
  4. Legal risks. Property may be under seizure, have encumbrances or ownership disputes. It is important to check the legal status of the property before buying and consult lawyers, or work with trusted licensed companies that check every property.
  5. Political risks. Political instability and changes in legislation, taxation or regulation can affect the real estate market and lead to higher costs or a lower return.
  6. Financial risks. These relate to insufficient liquidity of the property, changes in lending conditions or exchange-rate fluctuations. If a property is hard to sell or to pledge as collateral, it can be harder to get the money back.

Understanding and analysing these risks will help you make better-informed decisions. It is important to always take steps to minimise them.

How to assess the level of risk

When investing in real estate, it is important not only to understand the potential risks but also to be able to quantify them. This allows more accurate planning and management of investments. Here is how to assess the level of risk:

  • Location. It affects the price and yield of the property. Study local market trends, population growth, jobs and infrastructure projects that drive changes in value.
  • Quality of tenants. A strong rent roll reduces the risk of losing income if the property becomes vacant.
  • Financial risks. A high debt burden can lead to payment problems if income from the property falls. Use the debt coverage ratio to understand how resilient the investment is.
  • Condition of the property. Old buildings often require significant repair and maintenance costs. Analysing the main systems (roof, plumbing, electrics) helps predict potential expenses.

Do not forget the general economic conditions either. Interest rates and inflation strongly affect the return on real estate. Sensitivity analysis is used to assess the impact of these factors.

Is investing in real estate risky?

Real estate is considered one of the most reliable ways to invest capital for many reasons. Here are the key factors that make it a safe asset:

  1. Physical backing. Real estate is a physical asset that can be used, improved and controlled. Unlike digital or financial assets, it has real value, which makes it stable and reliable.
  2. Long-term growth. Historically, the real estate market tends to grow over the long term. Even after short-term fluctuations, prices tend to recover and rise over time. A long-term investment can provide steady capital growth.
  3. Rental income. Residential or commercial properties can provide a stable cash flow. Rent is an attractive source of passive income.
  4. Portfolio diversification. Real estate lets you diversify your investment portfolio and reduce the risks associated with other types of assets.

The right choice of properties and strategies, and cooperation with reliable partners, will help you achieve good results and protect your investment.

What guarantees are available when investing in real estate

When investing in real estate, it is important to consider the various guarantees that help ensure the safety and reliability of your investment.

  • Developer guarantees. Completion of construction is one of the key guarantees developers often offer. If the project is delayed, the developer may offer compensation for additional costs or discounts on future purchases.
  • Management company guarantees. When the property is rented out, management companies can provide a number of guarantees that help ensure stable income and efficient management.
  • Rental guarantee. This includes the management company's obligation to keep the property occupied. In some cases, the company can also guarantee a minimum rental income, which reduces the risks of vacancies and uncertain income.
